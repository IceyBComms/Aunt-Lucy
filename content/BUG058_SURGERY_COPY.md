# BUG058_SURGERY_COPY.md — the surgery occasion

✅ **APPROVED BY KATE, Sunday 23 August 2026.** ⏭️ **Builds AFTER #033, so its two lift suggestions land on a lift task that works.**
🔴 **Top of the blocking list: Kate's friend is having a planned procedure, and the product's invite currently tells thirty friends she *"hasn't been well lately"*.**

---

## 🔑 The insight the set turns on

> **This is the one occasion where the person is currently WELL, the date is KNOWN, and the hard part is AFTERWARDS.**

⚖️ **The opposite of illness in every way that matters to the words. Illness is ongoing and uncertain; a procedure is bounded, scheduled, and can be organised IN ADVANCE — which is the best thing this product can do for it.**

---

## ✅ THE APPROVED COPY

### 1. Picker label
> **Surgery or a procedure**

### 2. The everyday invite line
> **got a medical procedure coming up**

✅ **Kate's wording, and it's better than Cowork's *"got surgery coming up"*: vague enough to cover a day procedure through to something serious overnight, and — the part that matters — it doesn't force her to disclose how bad it is to thirty people.**

⚠️⚠️ **GRAMMAR TRAP FOR WHOEVER BUILDS THIS. Kate wrote *"has a medical procedure coming up"*. The existing lines are FRAGMENTS that get interpolated — illness is `not been well lately`, new baby is `just welcomed their new baby`. If the template renders *"[Name]'s …"*, then *"has a medical procedure"* produces *"Mel's has a medical procedure coming up."*** ❗ **The stored fragment must be `got a medical procedure coming up`, unless the template turns out to be *"[Name] …"*, in which case `has` is right.** 📌 **Check the interpolation and show the RENDERED SENTENCE, not the fragment.**

### 3. The trusted line
> **getting a bit of help around a procedure and the weeks after**

✅ **Deliberately spans both sides — it doesn't assume the procedure has or hasn't happened, matching the care already in the existing illness trusted line.**

### 4. Task suggestions — FOUR, per Kate
> **A meal, left at the door**
> **A lift to an appointment**
> **A hand with the groceries**
> **A short visit, once I'm up to it**

⚖️ **Kate cut Cowork's six to four and dropped the school run. Right call — bereavement gets four for the same reason, and a recipient with kids will add it herself. A starting list that looks like a roster defeats the point.**
📌 ***"once I'm up to it"* is protective: visitors arriving too early is a real problem after a procedure, and this lets her say so without having to say it.**
📌 **One lift task, not two, because #033 gives it a three-way control — drop off, wait and bring home, or pick up.**

### 5. The gift delivery email paragraph
> **A procedure is one thing; the weeks afterwards are another. [Giver] has set up a page so the people around you can have the lifts, the meals and the shopping sorted before you're home — without you having to ask for any of it.**

⚠️ **Cowork tried and rejected *"the operation is the short part"* — truer of a knee than of cancer, and this line has to hold for both.**

### 6. Note placeholders — both tiers
> **Workplace:** *e.g. From all of us at Brightpath — good luck with it all. Work's here when you're ready, not before.*
> **Consumer:** *e.g. Thinking of you. Put me down for a lift — I'm around all week.*

✅ **Consumer models claiming ONE thing, the job *"let me take the first dinner"* does on the baby set — and it names a task that now exists.** ✅ **Workplace offers care and explicitly not help, per the care-vs-help rule; *"not before"* quietly gives her permission to stay away, which is what employees actually worry about.**
